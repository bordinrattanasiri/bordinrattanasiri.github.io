July 2026 · one lesson, measured

Eleven of eighteen questions landed on one step.

Not because it was the hard step. Because it was four missing steps wearing one number — and nothing in a chat answer could have shown that, to me or to the model.

I teach music theory in Thailand — fundamentals through harmony, counterpoint and form, to students from complete beginners up to university level. Fifteen years of it. My own mathematics stopped in secondary school.

I asked Claude to teach me derivatives, a topic I had never studied. The explanation it gave in chat was good. Good enough that I asked for it back word for word as a web page, with two buttons on every sub-step: one to mark it understood, and one that opens a box to type a question about that step. Whatever I asked came back as a panel attached to the step itself, so the answer sat where the confusion was. Claude cut its own answer into 25 steps; I did not choose that number, or the places it cut.

Nothing else changed. Same words, same model, same session, a few minutes apart. The only new thing was somewhere to point.

These are measurements of the chat answer, not of the page. The page kept every word; it only made the seams visible. Cut along its own conceptual joints, an explanation that read as complete averaged 223 characters per step — and three times its entire length had to be written again before I could get through it.

A learner cannot name what they do not understand; that is what not understanding is. Given somewhere to point, they can point, and the shape of where they point is the missing curriculum.

Everything under it — where the formula came from, what a ramp has to do with gravity, what an acceleration unit means, why seconds appear twice — was ground the step stood on and did not lay. That is not a hard step. It is four missing steps wearing one number.

What went into the skills

Three rules in the current skills exist because of what is on this page, and each one costs real work to obey:

  • A cluster of questions on one step means a section is missing, not that the step is hard. The repair is never a longer answer. It is new steps on the main line, in the next lesson.
  • Completeness cannot be deferred to questions. Everything that made this lesson land ended up inside answer windows, invisible to anyone who did not press a button. So the first delivery has to carry it instead.
  • A step whose prose fits in one paragraph is an outline entry, not a step. Twenty-one of twenty-five steps here were under 600 characters. Write it properly or fold it into its neighbour.

One lesson, one learner, one subject. This is a worked case that produced rules, not a controlled study, and it is offered as the former. What it does show cleanly is the container effect: the words were identical on both sides, and only one of them could reveal where it was thin.
